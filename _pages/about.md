## Archives, Systems, and Access

**My work examines how archival information is structured, preserved, and made available—and how the systems we design shape its reliability, interpretation, and use.**

I am a digital archivist, curator, and information science researcher specializing in **archival informatics, digital archival systems architecture, and research infrastructure**. My practice connects archival theory with the design and implementation of systems for complex, distributed collections, from preservation infrastructure and metadata models to workflow automation, AI-assisted discovery, and public access.

I bring more than fourteen years of experience working with congressional, governmental, audiovisual, and cultural heritage collections. Across these settings, my work has centered on maintaining provenance and context while developing practical ways to organize, preserve, connect, and study archival materials at scale.

## Research and Applied Practice

My research begins with a question: **what must happen within an archival information system for information to remain reliable as it moves from creation and acquisition through preservation, description, and access?**

I study the points at which information loses context, workflows introduce inconsistencies, or systems become difficult to sustain. From there, I develop and test metadata structures, point-of-creation controls, feedback mechanisms, and adaptive learning methods intended to improve the reliability and interoperability of archival processes.

This work brings together **digital preservation, metadata architecture, workflow design, AI-assisted processing, and large-scale digital asset management**. I am particularly interested in the relationship between computational methods and archival judgment: how automated processes can remain accountable to source materials, how corrections can inform subsequent processing, and how systems can document the decisions that shape description and discovery.

My research and professional practice inform one another. Questions arising from collection management guide the development of methods and tools; implementation provides opportunities to examine those methods in relation to institutional requirements, staff knowledge, and the characteristics of particular collections.

## Consulting and Systems Development

My independent consulting practice extends this research into the planning, development, and evaluation of digital archival environments. I work with archives, libraries, museums, universities, and collaborative collecting initiatives on projects that require both archival expertise and technical implementation.

Areas of practice include:

- **Digital archival systems architecture:** Requirements analysis, repository design, system integration, migration planning, and workflows connecting acquisition, preservation, description, and access.
- **Digital content and research environments:** Development of digital collections, research portals, online exhibits, and discovery tools that connect archival materials with their historical and institutional contexts.
- **Preservation infrastructure:** Design of storage, ingest, backup, fixity verification, and preservation-event workflows for born-digital and digitized collections.
- **Metadata and knowledge organization:** Metadata models, crosswalks, controlled vocabularies, name authority structures, and entity-resolution workflows supporting consistent description and cross-collection research.
- **Automation and AI-assisted methods:** Scripting, API integration, text extraction, data normalization, and evaluation of machine-assisted processing with human review and documented quality controls.
- **Documentation and institutional continuity:** Policies, operating procedures, training, and technical handoff that support sustained stewardship beyond a project's initial development.

Engagements may involve a focused assessment, the design and implementation of a defined system, or an extended appointment supporting development, research, and institutional transition.

## Professional Background

I previously served as **Lead Archivist III and Curator at the Heritage and Research Center at Saint Mary’s (HARC), 2025–2026**. As one of its two founding archivists, I developed policies, processing workflows, metadata models, and digital discovery tools for a collaborative repository representing **ten congregations of Catholic women religious and their sponsored ministries**. This work addressed the challenge of building shared archival systems while maintaining the provenance and institutional context of separately created collections.

At the **Carl Albert Congressional Research and Studies Center Archives, University of Oklahoma**, I served as **Senior Archivist III and Curator, 2018–2025**, and taught information science. My responsibilities included stewardship of approximately **175.5 TB of digital congressional assets**, electronic-records program development, repository migration, preservation workflows, and collaborative research. Earlier roles at the **Oklahoma Historical Society** involved statewide digitization, born-digital records, and audiovisual preservation.

I hold a **Ph.D. in Information Science**, with a focus on archival informatics, an **M.L.I.S.**, with a focus on digital content management, and an **M.S. in Museum Science**.

## Selected Work

### [Adaptive Learning Models for Archival Systems](https://prys0000.github.io/adaptive-learning-models/)

My archival informatics research examines how adaptive learning, structured feedback, and point-of-creation controls can improve archival workflows. It considers how machine-assisted processes can operate within documented archival systems, incorporate correction, and support human decision-making. This research grew from large-scale processing and digitization projects and extends to questions of standardization, reliability, and information movement across systems.

### [American Congress Digital Archives Portal](https://congressarchives.org/)

My congressional archives work included collaborative development of the American Congress Digital Archives Portal, a multi-institutional effort to expand access to congressional collections and support their use in research, civics, and history education. My contributions centered on archival workflows, digital preservation, metadata, interoperability, processing methodology, and approaches to shared discovery.

### [HARC Archives and Digital Discovery](https://harc-archives.libraryhost.com/s/purple/page/welcome)

During my appointment at HARC, I developed public research pathways connecting **finding aids, digital collections, a Sisters Names Index, collection browsing, research resources, and AI-assisted archival discovery**. I also designed metadata and preservation workflows intended to support research across congregational collections while retaining the context of their creation. This work formed part of my broader investigation of name identification, archival relationships, and cross-institutional discovery.

### [Archives Handwriting Text Extraction and Analysis](https://github.com/prys0000/archives-handwriting-text-extract-project)

This project explores adaptable methods for extracting and improving text from handwritten archival documents. It examines how workflows can accommodate particular writers, scripts, names, abbreviations, and recurring language, and how repositories can adapt these methods to their collections and technical environments.

### [Political Campaign Advertisement Research](https://github.com/prys0000/political-commercial-collection-archives)

This NSF-supported collaborative research uses historical political campaign advertisements to study changes in political communication. My contributions focused on the archival, metadata, preservation, and computational infrastructure needed to prepare a complex audiovisual collection for research and analysis.
